For most practices, Google is still the single biggest source of new patients. But "ranking on Google" for a doctor is really two systems working together: **organic Search** and the **Maps / local pack**.

## The two surfaces that matter

### Google Search

The classic blue links. For a doctor, this is your website, your directory profiles, and any articles that mention you. Structured data and strong content help here.

### Google Maps & the local pack

The map with three highlighted businesses that appears for "dentist near me". This is driven by your **Google Business Profile**, reviews, and proximity to the searcher.

## The local ranking signals

Google has been fairly open that the local pack is driven by three factors:

| Factor | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| Relevance | How well your profile matches the search |
| Distance | How close you are to the searcher |
| Prominence | How well-known and well-reviewed you are |

You cannot change distance — but relevance and prominence are entirely within your control.

## A prioritized checklist

- Claim and fully complete your **Google Business Profile**
- Choose the most specific primary category for your specialty
- Add photos, hours, services, and insurance accepted
- Ask satisfied patients for reviews — and reply to every one
- Keep your name, address, and phone identical everywhere (NAP consistency)
- Add `MedicalOrganization` structured data to your website

> Prominence compounds. A steady trickle of fresh, replied-to reviews beats a one-time burst every time.
